About

I like getting close enough to a complicated problem that it starts to become workable.

I’m Victor, a software developer with experience in Dynamics 365 F&O and ERP. My work spans requirements, technical design, implementation, testing, deployment and support, with cross-team coordination along the way.

That end-to-end view matters to me. A requirement is connected to somebody’s day; an implementation is connected to its next change. I want to understand those connections, not just the task in front of me.

Outside that work, I’m curious about small computers, home servers and the ways AI can help with processes and web applications. This is a place to put that professional practice and personal curiosity beside each other.

The practice trail

Responsibilities, not a dated employment history.
Open a stage for a closer look.

  1. 01

    Requirements

    Understand the work before changing the software.

    I work through requirements with the people closest to the problem. The point is to understand the business need, not just receive a list of features.

    Read the responsibility

    A useful conversation makes the expected behaviour explicit: who needs it, what should happen, and where the current process gets in the way. I want those decisions to be understandable to both the people using the system and the people building it.

  2. 02

    Technical design

    Turn an understood need into a workable approach.

    My grounding is in Dynamics 365 F&O and ERP. Technical design connects business requirements to an implementation that can be tested, deployed and supported.

    Read the responsibility

    I think about the whole path rather than the code in isolation. An approach needs to fit its surrounding system, explain its assumptions and leave room for the testing and handover that come later. The design is a conversation with implementation, not a document to file away.

  3. 03

    Implementation

    Build with the next person in mind.

    I take part in turning the technical approach into working software. That means paying attention to the intent of the requirement as well as the detail of the implementation.

    Read the responsibility

    I care about making the complicated workable: understandable behaviour, changes that can be reasoned about, and a clear connection between the request and the result. I do not treat the first version of the code as the end of the responsibility.

  4. 04

    Testing & deployment

    Follow the change beyond the development environment.

    Testing and deployment are part of my end-to-end scope. A change has to make sense in use, not only while it is being built.

    Read the responsibility

    I work through expected behaviour and the steps needed to move a change into its intended environment. Clear checks, shared expectations and a considered handover help connect development with the people who will live with the software.

  5. 05

    Support & coordination

    Stay connected to what happens next.

    Support and cross-team coordination complete the chain. Questions after deployment can clarify the original need and inform the next change.

    Read the responsibility

    Software sits inside somebody’s working day. I value the conversations between users, developers and other teams that keep a problem from getting lost at a handoff. Support is not an unrelated final task; it feeds back into understanding the work.

Things I keep
coming back to

Small machines, useful possibilities

Raspberry Pi, homelab and server interests. I like the idea of learning by bringing software closer to the hardware it runs on. This is an area of curiosity, not a claim of a particular installation or measured result.

AI, with a useful job to do

I’m interested in AI-assisted work processes and web-app experiments: where it can help a person move a task forward, and where judgment still needs to stay with that person. These are interests to explore, not public client case studies.
